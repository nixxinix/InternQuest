## Before you apply {#before}

- Read the whole listing, including the requirements and the deadline.
- Check that the location, hours, and dates can work for you and your school.
- Have your resume ready as a PDF. See the [resume tips](/tips/resume).

You can still apply if you're missing a skill or two. Listings often describe the ideal person, and companies know interns are still learning.

## Cover letter structure {#cover-letter}

Keep it to three short paragraphs. Write a new first paragraph for every company, and reuse the rest with small changes.

1. **Why them.** Name the role and one specific reason you want to work there.
2. **Why you.** One or two things you've done that match what they need. A project works well here.
3. **The ask.** Say you'd like to talk, mention your OJT hours and when you can start, and thank them.

> Keep it under a page. A short, specific letter beats a long, general one.

## Applying by email {#email}

- **Subject:** say what it is, for example "OJT application: IoT Firmware Intern – Mika Reyes".
- **Body:** a short greeting, two or three lines on who you are and what you're applying for, and a thank-you. Or paste your cover letter.
- **Attachments:** your resume as a PDF with a clear file name. Add other files only if the listing asks for them.
- Send it from an email address with your name in it.

## Messaging a recruiter {#recruiter}

If you're reaching out on LinkedIn, Facebook, or by email, keep it short and polite:

> "Hi! I'm a 3rd-year IT student from Quezon City looking for a web development OJT. I saw your listing for a Frontend Intern and applied today. I'd be happy to share more about my projects. Thank you!"

Don't send the same message to many people at the same company, and don't message late at night.

## Following up {#follow-up}

If you haven't heard back after about a week or two, a short follow-up is fine:

> "Hi! I applied for the Data Analyst OJT on November 3 and wanted to check if there are any updates. I'm still very interested. Thank you for your time!"

Follow up once or twice at most. If there's still no reply, mark it as "No reply" in your quest log and keep going. It's not about you; companies get busy.

## Spotting fake listings {#scams}

Real internships never ask you to pay. Be careful if a listing:

- asks for money for training, uniforms, IDs, a medical exam, or to "reserve a slot";
- asks for your bank or e-wallet login, or a one-time PIN;
- promises a very high allowance for very little work;
- only gives a personal messaging account and no company details.

If something feels off, don't send money or IDs. Use **Report this listing** on InternQuest and we'll look into it.

## Keeping track {#tracking}

Applications are easy to lose track of. Every time you apply, mark it in your quest log. It reminds you when to follow up and shows how far you've come. For applications you sent outside InternQuest, use "Add one from elsewhere".
