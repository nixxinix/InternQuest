title: Interview tips
summary: Prepare a few stories, ask good questions, and follow up after.

## Before the day {#before}

Most of an interview is decided by what you do the day before. Give yourself an hour.

- Read the listing again and note the three skills they care about most.
- Read your own resume. Anything on it can come up.
- Prepare three short stories about things you did (the STAR method below helps).
- Write down two or three questions to ask them.
- Check the time, the place or link, and who you're meeting.

## Research the company {#research}

You don't need to know everything, just enough to show you care.

- What do they make or do, and who are their customers?
- What would an intern actually do there? The listing usually says.
- Look at their website and recent posts. Note one thing you found interesting.

> A simple line like "I saw you're working on solar charge controllers, and I'd love to help test them" goes a long way.

## Common internship questions {#questions}

- **Tell me about yourself.** Keep it to about a minute: your course, what you enjoy, and why this internship.
- **Why do you want to intern here?** Use what you found in your research.
- **Tell me about a project you're proud of.** Pick one where you can explain your own part.
- **Describe a time you had a problem in a group.** Focus on what you did to fix it.
- **What are your strengths and weaknesses?** Be honest. For a weakness, say what you're doing about it.
- **How many OJT hours do you need, and when can you start?** Know your school's requirement and dates.
- **Do you have questions for us?** Always say yes.

## The STAR method {#star}

STAR is a simple way to tell a story about something you did:

1. **Situation.** Where were you and what was happening?
2. **Task.** What did you need to do?
3. **Action.** What did **you** do? This is the longest part.
4. **Result.** What happened in the end, and what did you learn?

Example: "Our capstone sensor kept giving wrong readings (situation). I had to find out why before our demo (task). I tested each part separately and found a loose ground wire, then redid the wiring on a proper board (action). The readings were stable for the demo, and I now test parts one at a time first (result)."

## Questions to ask them {#ask}

- What would a normal day look like for an intern?
- Who would I be working with, and who would guide me?
- What would make an intern successful here?
- Can you sign my school's MOA and track my OJT hours?
- Is there an allowance, and what are the working hours?

## Online interviews {#online}

- Test the app (Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams) the day before.
- Find a quiet spot with light in front of you, not behind you.
- Use earphones with a mic if you have them.
- Keep your phone charged as a backup. If the call drops, rejoin and say what happened.
- Look at the camera when you talk, not at your own video.

## What to wear {#wear}

When in doubt, dress one step neater than what people at the company wear every day. Clean, simple clothes are always fine. If you're unsure, it's okay to ask the person who set up the interview.

## After: the follow-up message {#follow-up}

On the same day or the next, send a short thank-you. It's polite, and it keeps you in their mind.

> "Hi Ms. Santos, thank you for talking with me today about the IoT Firmware internship. I enjoyed learning about your field tests and I'm excited about the chance to help. Have a good day! – Mika"

If you haven't heard back after about a week or two, it's fine to ask politely about the status.

## If you get an offer {#offer}

Congratulations! Before you say yes, check:

- The start and end dates, and that the hours match what your school needs.
- Whether they'll sign your MOA and certify your hours.
- The allowance, if any, and how it's paid.
- Your schedule, and whether any of it is remote.
- Who your supervisor will be.

It's okay to ask for a day or two to decide.

## If it's a no {#rejection}

A "no" happens to everyone, and it isn't a judgment of your worth. It's still progress: you prepared, you showed up, and you got practice.

- Reply politely and thank them. Doors stay open.
- You can ask: "Is there anything I could improve for next time?" Some will answer.
- Write down which questions were hard, and prepare for them next time.
- Then pick your next listing.

- [ ] I prepared three STAR stories {#star-stories}
- [ ] I practiced "Tell me about yourself" out loud {#practiced}
- [ ] I wrote down two questions to ask them {#questions-ready}
- [ ] I know my OJT hours and possible start date {#hours}
- [ ] I checked the time, place or link, and who I'm meeting {#logistics}

- [ ] I sent a thank-you message after {#thank-you}
